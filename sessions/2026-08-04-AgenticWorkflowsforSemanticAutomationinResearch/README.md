### Session Title
> From Unstructured Text to Knowledge Graphs: Agentic Workflows for Semantic Automation in Research

### Presenter(s)
> Tim Nguyen, ARC Centre for Behavioural Insights for Technology Adoption (ARC BITA) at QUT

### Abstract
> Many valuable research collections consist primarily of narrative documents rather than analysis-ready datasets. Information about people, events, institutions, locations and relationships may be scattered across passages and documents, making manual coding costly, difficult to reproduce and hard to scale.
>
> This session introduces an agentic semantic automation workflow for transforming unstructured text into structured, queryable research data and knowledge graphs. Tim will explain how research questions can be translated into schemas that define entities, attributes, relationships, data types, provenance and uncertainty. The session will then demonstrate how specialised AI agents can coordinate document extraction, schema-guided analysis, entity resolution, data validation and graph generation. It will also cover agent tracing, input/output monitoring, cost tracking and approaches to human review. Attendees will leave with a practical understanding of how these workflows can support reproducible research, interactive inquiry and network analysis while preserving links between extracted claims and their original source evidence.

### Learning outcomes
> By the end of the session, attendees should be able to:
> 
> - Explain how semantic automation converts narrative documents into structured research data. 
> - Translate a research question into a basic schema of entities, attributes and relationships. 
> - Describe how specialised agents, tools and handoffs can support a multi-stage extraction workflow. 
> - Identify key validation, provenance and human-review requirements. 
> - Understand how structured outputs can be converted into knowledge graphs for querying and network analysis.

### Who should attend
> - Researchers, research assistants, data scientists, digital humanities scholars, software developers, data engineers and analysts working with document collections, qualitative material, archival sources or other unstructured research data.

### Prerequisites & setup
> - No software installation or prior coding experience is required. 
> - Some familiarity with language models, research data or network analysis may be helpful but is not essential.
